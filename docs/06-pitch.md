# Pitch

## One-liner (25 words max)

- **Recommended, 22 words:** "Cursor for CAD: we turn engineers' drawing markups into finished model changes, so electrical firms spend hours, not days, on each revision."
- **Without the Cursor name, 21 words:** "We automatically turn engineers' drawing markups into CAD model changes, so electrical engineering firms spend hours, not days, on each revision."
- **Pain first, 23 words:** "Engineers lose days redrawing marked-up plans by hand. We make every requested change in their CAD model automatically, and they approve each one."

None of them say "Revit" or "Bluebeam", because many judges won't know those names.

## 60-second Startmate pitch (29 Sep 2026)

> I'm Connor. Last week I spent all 16 hours of my engineering job making changes senior engineers had marked up on drawings, by hand, in CAD. At my last job, six of us interns did the same. At one firm, juniors must do two years of CAD before they can progress.
>
> Yet nine in ten markups say exactly what to do. It's translation, not engineering.
>
> We're building Cursor for CAD. It reads the markups and makes the changes in the model, to the firm's standards, and an engineer approves each one. Days of drafting become hours of checking.
>
> Existing tools find each markup, then leave most of the work to you.
>
> Firms pay a monthly fee per office, less than one week of a junior's salary. We start with electrical firms in Australia, then every discipline, everywhere engineers draft.
>
> Every junior engineer we've asked has this problem. Next: a working demo for ten firm owners by November.

157 words, which is 60 seconds at a brisk pace. If it runs over, cut the "existing tools" line first. Never cut the fee line: a pitch without a business model scores zero for it.

Startmate says it scores founder connection to the problem, scale of ambition and evidence of customer engagement. The pitch covers those and the other things judges usually expect:

| Judges want to hear | Covered by |
|---|---|
| The founder's link to the problem | "Last week I spent all 16 hours..." |
| That it's widespread | Six interns, and the two-years-of-CAD firm |
| Why it can be automated | "Nine in ten... translation, not engineering" |
| The product | "It reads the markups and makes the changes..." |
| Competition | "Existing tools find each markup..." |
| How we make money | "A monthly fee per office..." |
| Ambition | "Electrical firms in Australia, then every discipline, everywhere" |
| Customer evidence and next step | "Every junior engineer we've asked..." and the November goal |

## Customer problem statement (draft, 28 Sep 2026)

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Our customer is** | Small electrical engineering firms (under 20 staff per office) that do their own Revit drafting, starting with comms and security firms on government projects. |
| **Who is trying to** | turn senior engineers' marked-up drawings into an updated Revit model and sheets, to the firm's own standards, before the drawings are due to be issued. |
| **But struggles because** | every markup is done by hand, one at a time. Each takes about 20 minutes, mostly finding the spot in the model and fitting the part onto the right wall or ceiling. One of us spent all 16 work hours of a week in September 2026 on it, and existing tools still leave most of it by hand. |
| **And will still switch because** | about 9 in 10 markups say exactly what to do, so they're paying engineers for mechanical work while real design waits. And switching is low-risk. It works inside the Revit and Bluebeam they already use, with their own families and standards. Nothing leaves the building, which matters on secure government jobs, and an engineer approves every change. |

The "starting with" clause is optional: it is where the evidence comes from, and where keeping drawings in the building matters most.

## Questions to be ready for

- **"Cursor tried CAD and quit. Why will you work?"** Cursor's founders first built AI autocomplete for mechanical CAD, then moved to code by mid-2022: they weren't mechanical engineers, there was little usable CAD training data, and they had picked the market because it looked quiet. Answer: "Cursor tried to get AI to design parts, with no engineers on the team and no data. I do this job every week, and we don't design anything: we apply the changes engineers have already marked up." Sources: [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/who-is-michael-truell-cursor-ceo-spacex-acquisition/).
- **"Where do your numbers come from?"** The 16 hours and the six interns are Connor's own experience, the two years came from one junior engineer, and nine in ten is Connor's estimate from the 28 Sep 2026 interview. A counted tally of one markup set would make it firmer.
- **"Isn't MarkupX already doing this?"** "The closest tool out there finds each markup and drops the part in, then leaves you to fit it onto the wall or ceiling by hand, one at a time. We do the whole set, fitting included."
- **"Can you trust AI with drawings?"** Standard markups use no AI at all. AI only proposes changes for free-form notes, and the engineer approves every change.
- **"What about secure projects?"** It runs locally inside the firm's own Revit, and secure projects never touch the cloud.

## Keep the cost stories separate

At a firm with juniors, the markups are junior time; at small offices they are senior time. Do not mix the two numbers.
